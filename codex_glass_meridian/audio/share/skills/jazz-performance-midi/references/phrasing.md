# Phrase-aware post-bop playback

At medium-up tempos, swing can emerge from quarter-note placement, articulation, accents and interaction without a rigid 2:1 subdivision. Avoid treating every pair of eighths as a triplet long-short, accenting every upbeat, or resetting the phrase at each barline. Unstructured timing jitter does not create convincing swing.

For a lightly swung modern line, an initial second-eighth placement around 52–60% through the beat can be useful. This is a starting range, not a rule or historical claim. Flowing runs can approach even eighths; shorter gestures can lean more. Make the placement vary smoothly with phrase direction and arrival rather than drawing a new ratio for every note. Preserve explicitly notated triplets, tuplets and syncopations.

Let the bass establish a stable quarter-note foundation. Horns and piano can sit slightly differently around it—single-digit to low tens of milliseconds is a useful scale to explore at medium-up tempos. Keep purposeful anticipations separate from accidental misalignment. Do not apply the same displacement to every player.

For horns, sustained/legato samples can connect running eighths; short articulations suit selected accents and endings. Respect breathing spaces. Avoid a detached sample attack on every note and avoid slurring everything indiscriminately. Shape the approach to a peak and the release from it. Use continuous controllers only if the chosen instrument actually responds to them.

For drums, give the ride a quarter-note foundation with varied skip notes and intensity. Let snare responses follow phrase openings and pressure points. Avoid an identical fill every eight bars, perpetual loud backbeats, or a uniformly hard kick. Physical feet on 2 and 4 can anchor the groove while the hands vary. Choose the amount of interaction to match the arrangement.

For contrapuntal solos, preserve the composed imitation, overlap and dropouts. A supporting voice can play under a sustained note, then take the foreground as the other drops out. Instrument replacement should preserve the original concert pitches and rests unless range or the user's request calls for another decision.

Check a head phrase, an exposed solo entrance, dense overlap, and a return to the head. Listening evaluation should address pulse, forward motion, line continuity and intelligibility—not just whether every note was rendered.
