# Device map and texture palette

## Chord type -> scale, triad pair, pentatonics

All examples are over a C root; every triad and pentatonic listed uses only notes of the scale
in the second column. "Bright tone" = the note that gives the chord its colour.

| Chord | Scale (over C) | Triad pair | Pentatonics that fit | Bright tone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| maj7♯11 | lydian: C D E F♯ G A B | I / II (C and D) | G major (G A B D E); D major (D E F♯ A B) adds the ♯11 | ♯11 |
| maj7♯5 | lydian augmented: C D E F♯ G♯ A B | II / III (D and E) | D major (D E F♯ A B) | ♯5, ♯11 |
| maj7, maj9 | ionian: C D E F G A B (or lydian) | V / vi (G and Am), or I / II for a lydian tint | C major, G major | 9, 13 |
| m7, m9, m11 | dorian: C D E♭ F G A B♭ | ♭III / IV (E♭ and F) | C minor, D minor, G minor | 6, 9 |
| m(maj7) | melodic minor: C D E♭ F G A B | ♭III+ / IV (E♭ augmented and F) | F major (F G A C D); C minor-6 (C E♭ F G A) | maj7 |
| 7♯11, 13♯11 | lydian dominant: C D E F♯ G A B♭ | I / II (C and D) | C major (C D E G A); add the D triad for the ♯11 | ♯11 |
| 7alt | altered: C D♭ E♭ E G♭ A♭ B♭ | ♭V / ♭VI (G♭ and A♭) | G♭ major (G♭ A♭ B♭ D♭ E♭ = ♯11 ♭13 ♭7 ♭9 ♯9) | ♭9, ♯9 |
| 7♭9 | half-whole diminished: C D♭ E♭ E F♯ G A B♭ | two major triads a minor 3rd apart (C and E♭, or C and A) | none | ♭9, 13 |
| 7sus, 13sus | mixolydian without the 3rd: C D F G A B♭ | ♭VII / IV (B♭ and F) | B♭ major (B♭ C D F G); F major (F G A C D) | 4, 9 |
| m7♭5 | locrian ♮2: C D E♭ F G♭ A♭ B♭ | ♭VI / ♭VII (A♭ and B♭) | A♭ major (A♭ B♭ C E♭ F) | ♮9 |

Transpose by interval, not by letter: the tritone-sub pentatonic over B7alt is F major.

Worked example (E♭maj7♯11 then D♭maj7♯5): the E♭/F triad pair is I/II of E♭ lydian and
II/III of D♭ lydian augmented, so one line keeps going across the bass move.

## Modern devices

- **Pentatonic shifting:** state a 4-note cell from one pentatonic, then the same shape in
  another (B♭ to F pentatonic over E♭maj7♯11 brings in the ♯11).
- **Side-slip:** move a short cell up a half step for one beat or group, then back (outside -
  inside). Flag it in the checker notes.
- **Rhythmic displacement series:** restate a motif 0, 1, 2 eighths later each time; or group
  in 3s (dotted quarters) or 5s (4 notes + 1 rest) against 4/4 so accents drift.
- **Triad pairs in triplets:** alternate the two triads in eighth-note triplets for a fast,
  bright line.
- **Quartal lines:** stacked 4ths from chord tones (D-G-C-F over E♭maj7♯11).
- **Guide-tone lines:** 3rds and 7ths moving by step through the changes; excellent as a slow
  counterline against a busy one.

## Classic vocabulary (use a little)

Bebop enclosures (above-below-target), 1-2-3-5 digital patterns (Coltrane), bebop triplet turn,
♭9 arpeggio from the 3rd over a dominant, "down the scale, up the arpeggio" ii-V, tritone-sub
arpeggio over V7, Wes-style octaves on guitar, trumpet falls and doits at phrase ends.

## Duo textures

| Texture | What it is | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Call and response | One phrase, then the answer | Openings, trading |
| Canon | Same line, second voice a beat or two later, maybe at the octave | Motif statements, recaps |
| Inversion in voice 2 | Second voice plays the motif upside down | Contrary motion without planning each note |
| Augmentation vs diminution | One voice has the motif in half notes, the other in eighths | Showing the theme two ways at once |
| Parallax / pedal | One voice holds a note while the other's line changes its meaning | Suspense before a section change |
| Hocket | One line split note by note between the players | A surprising texture, brief |
| Voice exchange | The two voices swap pitches | Quiet, closing moments |
| Contrary motion with crossing | Lines move toward and through each other | Energy, "weaving" |
| Octave unison | Both play the same line an octave apart | Climaxes, launches into a new section |
| Relay | A motif or cell handed back and forth each bar | Bridges with fast-moving keys |
