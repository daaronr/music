# Halation

Original straight-ahead post-bop tune plus a written two-chorus trumpet/guitar duo solo.
Medium-up swing, ♩ = 184, 32 bars (A B A' C), tonal home F lydian.

## The tune

**Changes (concert)**

| | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Fmaj7♯11 | Fmaj7♯11 | E♭maj7♯5 | E♭maj7♯5 | D7sus(♭9) | D7alt | Gm9 | C13sus |
| **B** | D♭maj7♯11 | Cm7 F7 | B♭maj7♯11 | B♭m7 E♭7 | Am7 D7 | A♭m7 D♭7 | Gm9 | C7alt |
| **A'** | Fmaj7♯11 | Fmaj7♯11 | E♭maj7♯5 | E♭maj7♯5 | Em7♭5 | A7(♭9) | Dm(maj7) | Cm7 F7 |
| **C** | B♭maj7♯11 | E♭9(♯11) | Am7 | D7alt | Gm9 | G♭7(♯11) | Fmaj7♯11 A♭maj7♯11 | D♭maj7♯11 G♭maj7♯11 |

- Bars 1–4 are really one G major triad floating over a falling bass (G/F, then G/E♭), so F lydian slides into E♭ lydian-augmented while the top stays put.
- B opens with a deceptive move from C13sus to D♭maj7♯11 (♭VI lydian), then a chromatically falling chain of ii–Vs, the straight-ahead heart of the tune.
- C ends with the "Lady Bird" turnaround with every chord made lydian.

**The idea behind the title.** Nearly every lydian chord's ♯11 is a white key: B over F, G over D♭, E over B♭, D over A♭, C over G♭, A over E♭9. The melody leans on *pivot tones*, one pitch recoloured as the harmony moves under it: B is the ♯11 of F and then the ♯5 of E♭ (bars 1–4); G is the 5th of C13sus and then the ♯11 of D♭ (8–9); C is the 11th of Gm and then the ♯11 of G♭7 (29–30). The turnaround melody (31–32) is simply the ♯11 of each chord: B, D, G, C.

**Motifs used throughout the solo**

- *Bloom*: rest on beat 1, then the ♯11 enters on the & of 1 and rings.
- *Z*: up a minor 3rd, down a 4th, up a 5th (A–C–G–D), built from the F and G triads (the lydian triad pair). The head restates it on D (bar 3), on G displaced to beat 2 (bar 5), and on the altered triad pair (B♭–D♭–A♭–E♭, bar 16).
- *Guide-tone line*: D♭–C–B–B♭ across the falling ii–Vs (bars 12–15).

**Form for performance:** head once; solos over the form (1st ending); head out; take the 2nd ending (Fmaj7♯11, fermata on the ♯11).

## The duo solo

Two choruses; trumpet and guitar enter, drop out, and overlap. C1/C2 = chorus 1/2, followed by bar numbers within the form.

**Citing and developing the melody**

- C1 1–2: the bloom a beat late, and Z pushed back an eighth, so Z's last note becomes a bloom.
- C1 5–8: head bars 5–8 displaced a full beat, still landing on the pivot G.
- C1 11, 23, 25–26, 30–32: the head's E bloom, the minor-major bloom, the climax, bar 30 with the guitar a 6th below, and the turnaround blooms (guitar triads fall to their roots, a tritone under each ♯11).
- C1 6–7 (guitar): Z in augmentation, in quarter notes over D7alt (C–E♭–B♭–F).
- C1 12–15 (trumpet): the head's guide-tone line against the guitar's bebop sequence, which deliberately leaves out the note the trumpet is holding.
- C2 43: guitar plays the head's bloom in octaves.
- C2 48: the head's altered Z in octaves, splitting into contrary motion.
- C2 51–52: Z in augmentation in the trumpet (D–F–C–G).
- C2 59–62: guitar quotes head bars 27–30 an octave down; trumpet counter-line, then a tenth above.
- C2 63–64: final blooms in parallel major 9ths.

**Counterpoint devices:** contrary motion (C1 3, 17–20, 25–26), hocket (C1 21–23, C2 44–46), octave soli (C1 24, C2 47–48), canon at two beats an octave down (C2 37–39), long-note cantus against triplet figuration (C2 49–52).

**Modern approaches**

- Triad pairs: F/G over Fmaj7♯11 and E♭maj7♯5 (C1 3–4, 17–20; C2 49–52), E♭/F over D7sus(♭9), A♭/B♭ over D7alt (C2 37–38), G/A over Dm(maj7) (C1 23).
- Pentatonic shifting: F major pentatonic to G♭ major pentatonic (out) and back (C2 34–36); one minor-pentatonic shape slid down a half step per bar over the falling ii–Vs, its ♭7 resolving to the next 3rd (C2 44–46).
- Rhythmic displacement: Z as a five-eighth-note cell that slides against the bar line (C2 33–36); three-note triad groupings (hemiola) against the guitar's dotted quarters (C1 17–20).

**A few classic licks:** "down the scale, up the arpeggio" ii–V (C1 10, guitar); altered descent with a G♭–E enclosure of F (C1 16); diminished arpeggio from the 3rd over A7(♭9) (C1 22); tritone-substitute (A♭7) arpeggio over D7alt (C1 28, guitar); B♭–G♯–A enclosure of the 3rd (C2 42).

**Peaks:** C1 25–26 up to concert A5 (written B5); C2 57–58 up to concert C6 (written high D). That C is the 9th of B♭maj7♯11, held into the 13th of E♭9(♯11): a last pivot tone.

**Ranges:** trumpet concert E4–C6 (written F♯4–D6, one high D); guitar sounding A2–D5, notated an octave higher in treble 8vb clef.

## The recording (`audio/Halation_band_demo.mp3`, 2:58)

Intro (last four bars of the form), head (trumpet with alto sax an octave below), the two written duo choruses with **alto sax playing the guitar line**, head out, and the fermata ending.

- **Trumpet:** real solo B♭ trumpet samples (University of Iowa Electronic Music Studios, mf and ff, non-vibrato), with swing phrasing, soft-tongued legato, delayed vibrato on long notes, and the written falls and doits.
- **Alto sax, piano, bass, drums:** Apple's GarageBand sampler instruments (Alto Sax, Steinway Grand, Upright Jazz Bass, Drum Kit Designer kit), played by a small numpy sampler (`src/audio/`).
- **Rhythm section:** generated from the changes, band-in-a-box style. Bass plays in two for the head's A sections and walks elsewhere, with chromatic approaches into each chord. Piano uses voice-led rootless voicings, lays out ("strolls") for the first eight bars of the duo, and comps more as chorus 2 builds. Drums play a jazz ride pattern with hi-hat on 2 and 4, snare comping, fills at section ends, a crash at the chorus-2 climax, and a cymbal swell on the ending.
- **Sax adjustments:** the guitar's chords and octaves become single notes, and two bars that dip below the alto's range (C1 19 and C2 52) are played an octave up.
- **Limits:** the drum kit is Apple's pop "SoCal" kit played with a jazz pattern (no brushes or jazz kit are installed), and there is no real-time human feel beyond humanised timing and velocity.

## Rebuilding

```bash
python3 src/build.py                 # notation: PDFs, .mscz, .musicxml, iReal chart (needs MuseScore 3)
python3 src/analyze.py all           # harmonic labels, ranges, counterpoint checks
bash src/audio/fetch_trumpet.sh      # once: download the Iowa trumpet samples into build/
/opt/homebrew/Caskroom/miniforge/base/bin/python3 src/audio/prepare_trumpet.py
/opt/homebrew/Caskroom/miniforge/base/bin/python3 src/audio/render.py
```

The music lives in `src/changes.txt`, `src/head.txt`, `src/solo_trumpet.txt` and `src/solo_guitar.txt` (concert/sounding pitch, simple text notation documented at the top of `src/musiclib.py`). `build.py` writes MusicXML, has MuseScore import it, patches in the MuseJazz house style and the exact chord spellings, and renders.
