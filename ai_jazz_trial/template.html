Can AI write a modern jazz tune?

One evening I gave the same composition prompt to three AI coding agents: two Claude Opus 5.5 sessions in Claude Code and one GPT-6 Astra session in Codex. Each got its own folder in my music repo, and the whole thing took about an hour. Each came back with an original tune and a two-chorus trumpet and guitar duo solo, as sheet music, MuseScore files and an iReal Pro chart. Two also made mocked-up recordings.

This is far from a clean trial, benchmark or comparison: one attempt per setup, different follow-up prompts, no blind listening, and nobody has played these yet. I’m sharing it because the results are interesting to read and, I hope, to play.

David Reinstein

$timelines

Setup

  • All three ran on my Mac in one Git repo that already had some of my chart work in it. Each session made its own subfolder and was told not to look at the others.
  • Claude: two separate sessions in the Claude Code desktop app, both Claude Opus 5.5 at max effort.
  • Codex: the Codex desktop app with GPT-6 Astra. Its log shows reasoning effort “xhigh” for the composition and the first recording, then “medium” and “high” for the audio fixes.
  • The machine had MuseScore 3.6.2, iReal Pro and Python. All three wrote their own code to generate the notation and rendered their PDFs through MuseScore.
  • My prompt pointed to “the folder/repo” for soloing ideas and then trailed off. The Parallax session went looking and found the topic list from my weekly brass-practice generator (pentatonic pairs, triad pairs, displacement).

Caveats

  1. One attempt per setup. The two Claude sessions share an opening move but otherwise came out quite differently, so a single run says little about a model.
  2. The follow-ups differed. Glass Meridian got four rounds of audio feedback, Halation one audio request and Parallax none, so the recordings aren’t comparable.
  3. Nobody has played these or rated them blind. The motif and device descriptions come from each run’s own notes; treat them as claims to check against the scores.
  4. The recordings are programmed sample performances, not people playing.
  5. This was an hour of me switching between windows, and the prompts have my typos in them.

If you play trumpet or guitar and try one of these, I’d like to hear how it sits on the instrument and whether the duo parts work as duets.
