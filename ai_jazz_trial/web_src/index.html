What this is

Round 1: one evening I gave the same composition prompt to three AI coding agents: two separate [[Claude Code]] sessions running Claude Opus 5.5 at [[max effort]], and one [[Codex]] session running GPT-6 Astra. Each worked in its own folder of my music repo, and the whole thing took about an hour, with a few follow-up prompts about audio. In November a fourth run joined round 1: Claude Fable 5.2 at max effort got the same prompt, word for word, unattended, in a copy of the repo as it was before the trial. It needed one “Continue.” after hitting a usage limit partway.

Round 2: a day later I wrote a darker brief (a jazz waltz, classical counterpoint in the duo, and a technique-by-technique solo etude) and gave it, word for word, to one Claude Opus 5.5 session and one GPT-6 Astra session, each run unattended under matched conditions. Round 2 is the cleaner comparison of the two; see How it was made.

This is far from a clean trial, benchmark or comparison. I’m sharing it because the results are interesting to read and, I hope, to play.

Caveats
  1. One attempt per setup. The two Claude sessions share an opening move but otherwise came out quite differently, so a single run says little about a model.
  2. The follow-ups differed, mostly about audio. The Codex run got four rounds of audio feedback; the other two got one audio request each. See How it was made.
  3. Nobody has played these or rated them blind. The descriptions of motifs and devices come from each run’s own notes; treat them as claims to check against the scores.
  4. The recordings are programmed sample performances, not people playing. After listening I had some things fixed by hand (a louder bass, a softer ride cymbal, less repetitive drums, bass and piano), always the same way for every tune in a round.
  5. This was an hour of me switching between windows, and the prompts have my typos in them.

Recordings: who got which audio request

Two sessions got one audio request each, in the same words: make an MP3 with something closer to [[Band-in-a-Box]] than “cheesy 8-bit MIDI,” and swap the guitar for alto sax on the recording (not the score). Halation’s session got it at 23:21 and made the Halation recording.

So that the round-1 tunes can be heard on equal terms, the headline recordings of Parallax, Glass Meridian and (in November) Brightwork were made by applying the Halation recording’s instructions unchanged: same code, samples, mix and random seed, each tune at its composer’s own tempo, with its own written ending.

On 28 September, after listening, I had the rhythm section redone for all five recordings on this page, in the same way: a softer ride cymbal, drums that change from bar to bar and phrase to phrase (fills, set-ups, a closed hi-hat or cross-stick on some sections), and bass lines and piano comping that repeat less. All three round-1 tunes were re-rendered with it, including Halation; its original demo is kept as an alternate on its card. The two round-2 recordings got the same change, and Brightwork was rendered with it from the start.

The Codex session got the first audio request at 22:52 (without the sax swap), then three more rounds of feedback: sound more natural, use sax instead of guitar, a less clichéd swing feel, and a fuller upright bass. Its own versions, v1 (trumpet and guitar) and v3 (trumpet and sax), are kept as alternates on the Glass Meridian card.

Setup
  • Round 1: all three ran on my Mac in one Git repo that already had some of my chart work in it. Each session made its own subfolder and was told not to look at the others.
  • Claude: two separate sessions in the Claude Code desktop app, both Claude Opus 5.5 at max effort.
  • Codex: the Codex desktop app with GPT-6 Astra. Its log shows reasoning effort “xhigh” for the composition and the first recording, then “medium” and “high” for the audio fixes.
  • The machine had MuseScore 3.6.2, iReal Pro and Python. All three wrote their own code to generate the notation and rendered their PDFs through MuseScore.
  • Brightwork (21 November): Claude Fable 5.2 at max effort, run unattended from the command line in a copy of the repo rebuilt as round 1 found it, with the skills written after round 1 switched off. MuseScore no longer ran on the Mac by then, so it engraved with LilyPond. Its first two attempts (14–15 November) failed on a network drop and a usage limit; the third ran 54 minutes, hit the 5-hour usage limit, and finished after one “Continue.” Its log shows no look at the other tunes.
  • My prompt pointed to “the folder/repo” for soloing ideas and then trailed off. The Parallax session went looking and found the topic list from my weekly brass-practice generator (pentatonic pairs, triad pairs, displacement).
