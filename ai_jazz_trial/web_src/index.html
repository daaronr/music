What this is

One evening I gave the same composition prompt to three AI coding agents: two separate [[Claude Code]] sessions running Claude Opus 5.5 at [[max effort]], and one [[Codex]] session running GPT-6 Astra. Each worked in its own folder of my music repo, and the whole thing took about an hour, with a few follow-up prompts about audio.

This is far from a clean trial, benchmark or comparison. I’m sharing it because the results are interesting to read and, I hope, to play.

Caveats
  1. One attempt per setup. The two Claude sessions share an opening move but otherwise came out quite differently, so a single run says little about a model.
  2. The follow-ups differed, mostly about audio. The Codex run got four rounds of audio feedback; the other two got one audio request each. See How it was made.
  3. Nobody has played these or rated them blind. The descriptions of motifs and devices come from each run’s own notes; treat them as claims to check against the scores.
  4. The recordings are programmed sample performances, not people playing.
  5. This was an hour of me switching between windows, and the prompts have my typos in them.

Recordings: who got which audio request

Two sessions got one audio request each, in the same words: make an MP3 with something closer to [[Band-in-a-Box]] than “cheesy 8-bit MIDI,” and swap the guitar for alto sax on the recording (not the score). Halation’s session got it at 23:21. Parallax’s was requested after this page went up; it follows the same request plus a written audio brief the Codex session produced.

The Codex session got the first audio request at 22:52 (without the sax swap), then three more rounds of feedback: sound more natural, use sax instead of guitar, a less clichéd swing feel, and a fuller upright bass. Its first version (v1) is the headline recording above, because it came from a single request like the others; v3 is under “other versions.” I’m also rendering all three tunes through one shared pipeline, so they can be heard on equal terms; those versions will go up here.

Setup
  • All three ran on my Mac in one Git repo that already had some of my chart work in it. Each session made its own subfolder and was told not to look at the others.
  • Claude: two separate sessions in the Claude Code desktop app, both Claude Opus 5.5 at max effort.
  • Codex: the Codex desktop app with GPT-6 Astra. Its log shows reasoning effort “xhigh” for the composition and the first recording, then “medium” and “high” for the audio fixes.
  • The machine had MuseScore 3.6.2, iReal Pro and Python. All three wrote their own code to generate the notation and rendered their PDFs through MuseScore.
  • My prompt pointed to “the folder/repo” for soloing ideas and then trailed off. The Parallax session went looking and found the topic list from my weekly brass-practice generator (pentatonic pairs, triad pairs, displacement).

If you play trumpet or guitar and try one of these, I’d like to hear how it sits on the instrument and whether the duo parts work as duets.
