# Brightwork

An original 32-bar tune (A1 A2 B A3, medium-up swing, quarter = 160, home key F lydian) with a written
two-chorus duo solo for trumpet and guitar. Composed by Claude (Fable 5.2) on 2026-11-21.

## The tune

```
A1 | Fmaj7#11  | Fmaj7#11  | Bm7  E7             | Amaj7     |
   | Abmaj7#11 | Dbmaj7#11 | Gm7                 | Gb7#11    |
A2 | Fmaj7#11  | Fmaj7#11  | Bm7  E7             | Amaj7     |
   | Abmaj7#11 | Dbmaj7#11 | F#m7                | B7        |
B  | Emaj7#11  | Emaj7#11  | Gmaj7#11            | Gmaj7#11  |
   | Bbmaj7#11 | Dbmaj7#11 | C7sus               | C7alt     |
A3 | Fmaj7#11  | Fmaj7#11  | Bm7  E7             | Amaj7     |
   | Abmaj7#11  Dbmaj7#11  | Gm7  C7 | Fmaj7#11  | Gbmaj7#11 |
```

- The hook is a lydian triad pair: F triad down, G triad up, landing on the 13th. The same six-note
  shape is restated on the ii-V into A major, then stretched out over A-flat and D-flat lydian using
  the upper triad of each pair (B-flat, then E-flat).
- The A section brightens (F lydian to A major), drops into shade (A-flat, D-flat lydian) and comes home.
- The bridge starts a half step below home, in E lydian, and climbs by minor thirds (E, G, B-flat,
  D-flat lydian). Each long melody note is the 13th of one chord and becomes the sharp 11 of the next.
  C7sus to C7alt brings it back.
- A3 plays the hook a third time in bar 29, compressed, and holds C over Fmaj7#11 then Gbmaj7#11.
- Head out: stop on bar 31 (Fmaj7#11) instead of going to Gbmaj7#11.

## The duo solo, section by section

The labels printed in the parts mark the same things. Bars are numbered 1-64.

| Bars | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1-8 (Ch.1 A1) | Trumpet opens alone with the hook displaced by three eighths. Guitar answers in canon an octave lower, one bar later, bent to fit the ii-V. Trumpet quotes the tail of the head (bar 4), then holds the sharp 11 and major 7 while the guitar climbs A-flat/B-flat triads and falls through E-flat/D-flat. Bar 7 is "the lick"; bar 8 is the head's cadence over the guitar's G-flat/A-flat triads. |
| 9-16 (A2) | Guitar leads (G/F triads down, then a ii-V line); trumpet comes in underneath with a rising guide-tone line. Bars 13-14: trumpet plays the hook "in the shade" on A-flat lydian and the guitar answers a fourth higher. Bars 15-16: a wedge. The lines start on a unison, move apart, then close in again on B7. |
| 17-24 (B) | Trumpet states the bridge theme while the guitar shifts pentatonics underneath (E, B, F-sharp). In bar 19 they swap: guitar takes the theme, trumpet plays a pentatonic in fourths. Bars 21-22: the triplet motif in stretto. Bars 23-24: stacked fourths over the sus chord, then the trumpet's altered run up against the guitar's line down. |
| 25-32 (A3) | Trumpet alone with the hook, pushed up to the ninth, then a bebop 3-5-7-b9 line into A. Guitar takes over with the compressed hook from bar 29 of the head, answers with its own 3-5-7-b9, then G pentatonic shifting to A-flat pentatonic over the turnaround, while the trumpet holds a slow chromatic line (A, B-flat, B). |
| 33-40 (Ch.2 A1) | Guitar plays the hook displaced by an eighth and carries it on in sequence. Trumpet: one pentatonic shape moved through the ii-V-I. Bars 37-38: the long-note version of the hook from the head, in parallel tenths. Bars 39-40: bebop scale and arpeggio; the guitar's triad ladder returns. |
| 41-48 (A2) | Trading that tightens: trumpet two bars (pentatonic, side-slip up a half step and back), guitar two bars (ii-V line, A/B triad pair), one bar each (triad pair in threes, answered a fifth lower), half a bar each, then a second wedge into the bridge. |
| 49-56 (B) | The climax. Trumpet turns the bridge motif into quarter-triplet arcs, each ending on the 13th, rising by minor thirds to concert A (written B above the staff). Guitar answers each arc with a triad-pair fill. Bars 55-56: both in octaves, sus pentatonic then altered pentatonic, and a beat of silence. |
| 57-64 (A3) | The hook comes back split between the two players (hocket). Lyrical trumpet over a moving guitar line, a last cadence in contrary motion with a bebop chromatic run-up, then the guitar's F/G and G-flat/A-flat triads under a held C. |

Ranges: trumpet concert C-sharp 4 to A5 (written D-sharp 4 to B5); guitar written B3 to F6 (sounding B2 to F5).

## Importing

- **MuseScore**: File > Open, choose a `.musicxml` file. Playback is straight until you add the Swing
  marking from the palettes (or set swing in the score style). The B-flat trumpet files are real
  transposing parts, so the Concert Pitch button works. The guitar is on a treble-8 clef.
- **iReal Pro**: open `ireal/Brightwork_iReal.html` on the device with iReal Pro and click the link.
  Style is Medium Up Swing, 160, key F.

## What was checked, and what was not

- Every bar adds up to four beats, and the pitches in each MusicXML file equal the source lines
  (after transposition for the B-flat parts): `src/verify.py`.
- All six MusicXML files validate against the official MusicXML 3.1 schema, and were read back with
  LilyPond's `musicxml2ly` as a second, independent reader.
- Notes against chords, instrument ranges and clashes between the two solo lines: `src/check.py`.
- The iReal link decodes back to the intended chart with the same routine that correctly decodes an
  existing iReal Pro export in this repo.
- Not done: opening the files in MuseScore itself (MuseScore 2 and 3 on this Mac are Intel builds and
  no longer run), and importing the link into iReal Pro (it would have added a song to your library).
- Nobody has played or heard this yet apart from the computer mock-up. Treat fingerings, breaths and
  articulation as yours to decide.

## Rebuilding

```
python3 src/build.py        # MusicXML, LilyPond, PDFs, iReal chart
python3 src/check.py        # musical sanity checks
python3 src/verify.py       # MusicXML checks (add the path to musicxml.xsd to validate the schema)
python3 src/demo_audio.py   # MIDI and MP3 mock-up
```

Needs LilyPond; the mock-up also needs fluidsynth, ffmpeg and a General MIDI soundfont.
The music lives in `src/music_head.py` and `src/music_solo.py`.
